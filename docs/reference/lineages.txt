LINEAGE DATA
============
Verified: 2026-09-09
Code: variants/

Two pipelines feed the same data structure. The live one pulls per-day sequence
counts for every US lineage from cov-spectrum's LAPIS API. The older one reads
CSV files a human exported from cov-spectrum's web UI by hand, and survives
because the open API does not carry the GISAID-backed data those pages showed.
Both produce a variants/Voc.java, and everything downstream is common.


ENTRY POINTS
------------
nwss/Nwss.java read() calls Voc.create() for the manual path and Lapis.create()
for the automatic one, and concatenates them; a run with no exported CSVs
present is the normal case. Each Voc is then paired with a sewage series in
variants/VocSewage.java, which is where fitting and merging happen, and charted
from there.


THE MANUAL PATH, AND WHY IT IS STILL HERE
-----------------------------------------
variants/LEnum.java hand-lists lineages; variants/LSet.java turns a list into
cov-spectrum comparison URLs and prints them; the maintainer opens those,
exports the resulting CSV to the downloads folder, and Voc.create() reads any
file matching the two expected names. Exports older than eight hours are
deleted rather than used, since a stale export silently describes last week.

GISAID's LAPIS is not public, so a query answered on cov-spectrum's site cannot
be answered by variants/Lapis.java. That is the whole reason this path is kept.
VocSewage getLink() and the LEnum loop in read() print the links; neither exits
the program any more, so a run that nobody is going to export from just prints
them and moves on.


THE LAPIS PATH
--------------
variants/Lapis.java asks the open (GenBank) LAPIS for US samples aggregated by
date and nextcladePangoLineage, and caches the JSON for 24 hours. Open data is
much thinner than GISAID, and thinning: 2,696 US sequences in January 2026 and
268-586 a month from April to July, against 7,737 in January 2025 (LAPIS,
2026-09-10; recent months still fill in). Most of the constants below exist
because of that thinness, and would want revisiting if the counts ever
recovered.

WINDOW_DAYS = 365. One year of history is emitted.

LAG_DAYS = 10. No prevalence is emitted for the last ten days. It matches what
the manual exports did (LSet.TODAY(-10)) and is not an attempt to model the
real reporting delay, which is longer -- around 26 days as of this writing. The
trailing-zero trim at the top of Voc build(), which drops trailing days on
which no lineage has any prevalence -- for LAPIS, days whose smoothing window
holds fewer than MIN_WINDOW_SEQUENCES sequences -- absorbs the remainder, so
the charts show a data cutoff where the data actually stops rather than where
this constant says.

SMOOTH = 3, a half-width, giving the 7-day centered average cov-spectrum
exported. Matching it is the point: the two paths must be comparable.

MIN_SEQUENCES = 20. A lineage with fewer than twenty sequences of its own over
the fetched days -- exact counts, plus whatever was already folded into it --
is folded into its parent before the Voc is constructed. This is input
curation and it changes no algorithm downstream. It exists because VocSewage's
own removal rule keeps anything with ten smoothed days, which two sequences
three days apart already produce -- a sane threshold when the input was a
hand-picked candidate list, and not when the input is every designated lineage.
Unfiltered, that rule kept 530 of 754. A lineage with no parent, a root, is
dropped instead; its sequences stay in the denominator and therefore surface in
"others". Few of its own does not make the family rare: on the data of
2026-09-10 the 22 roots dropped held 130 sequences, one of them XDV with 2 of
its own under a family of 2,050, and a root whose descendants survive is still
built as their ancestor.

MIN_WINDOW_SEQUENCES = 20. A day whose smoothing window holds fewer than twenty
sequences gets no prevalence at all. With a handful of sequences one lineage is
100% of the day, which is noise, and 100% is a value the logit axis cannot draw
(see docs/reference/charts.txt). "Others" gets none either: Voc build() gives it
a value only on a day some variant has prevalence on, so a thin day inside the
range is missing everywhere rather than drawn as 100% unnamed.


INCLUSIVE ROLL-UP: THE INVARIANT THE PIPELINE ASSUMES
-----------------------------------------------------
LAPIS counts are exact-lineage. The rest of the pipeline was written against
cov-spectrum "X*" queries, where a parent's number includes all its
descendants, and Voc build() subtracts children back out of ancestors deepest
first, using string-prefix ancestry over expanded names
(variants/Aliases.java isAncestorExclusive).

So Lapis must hand it inclusive counts: every lineage's daily counts are added
into every one of its ancestors, creating ancestors that had no sequences of
their own. Those end up as near-zero residuals after the subtraction and are
merged away later. Break this and the subtraction silently produces negative
prevalences.

Two smaller invariants hold it together. Counts stay integers until they are
divided, one division per lineage per day by that day's total, so the
subtraction is a/den - b/den and not exact -- what it is instead is small: the
shared denominator keeps the residual far under VocSewage MINIMUM. Replaying one
run's data -- a year of days, every lineage -- produces on the order of a
thousand tiny negatives, every one below that threshold, and Variant
subtractPrevalence drops those silently. A negative
big enough to warn about means the inclusive-counts invariant above is broken.

And a Variant only acquires a Lineage if its name matches
"nextcladePangoLineage:<alias>*" -- the regex in the Variant(String)
constructor -- which is exactly what Lineage.getQuery() emits. A variant with a
null lineage is never merged away, not subtracted from, and not assigned a
strain; "Others" is one, and it is the merge target for lineages with no
parent.


MERGING DOWN TO A LEGEND
------------------------
VocSewage build() repeatedly calls findLineageToRemove and folds what it
returns into its parent's variant, manufacturing that variant if it does not
exist, or into "Others" for a lineage with no parent. Each merge prints one
line saying which rule chose it and why. A pass has two tiers.

The floor comes first and works deepest first, so a subtree collapses from the
leaves inward. A lineage fails it when its fit has a non-finite slope, when the
fit's 60-day projection overflows (a two-point fit can be that steep, and it is
as unusable as no slope), or when, counted from its own fit start, it holds
under ten days of measurable prevalence or under 0.001 of sewage times
prevalence. The floor only asks whether
there is anything to fit. On the 0-100 sewage scale it removes little else,
which was fine when the input was a hand-picked list and is not when it is
every lineage LAPIS carries: on its own it left about 105 lineages on the
national chart and 197 on the Colorado one.

When nothing fails the floor, the cheapest merge is taken. Each lineage is
priced against its reference, the nearest ancestor still on the chart, as
c*p/(c+p) * ds^2: c and p are the two sizes (sewage times prevalence) over the
last 28 days of data, ds is the gap between their slopes. That is the
leading-order change the merge makes to the projection, so a lineage growing
like its ancestor and a tiny lineage are both cheap, and a big one diverging
from its ancestor is dear. ds is first shrunk by two standard errors of the two
slopes: at about one sequence a week per lineage most slope gaps are noise, and
a gap inside the noise costs nothing. Merging continues while more than
MAX_VARIANTS (10) remain; once ten or fewer do, it continues only while the
cheapest merge is free.

Two traps shaped this. The reference is the nearest ancestor on the chart and
not the merge target, because the direct parent is often manufactured empty
and anything priced against an empty variant is free, which would relabel every
lineage upward. And a lineage with no ancestor left on the chart -- a root, or
the top of a family whose ancestors were all folded away -- is referenced
against Others, and is not merged at all unless it has nothing in the last 28
days. In LAPIS data Others is nearly empty -- only the root sequences Lapis
drops, and any lineage name the pango checkout cannot resolve, land there -- so
every such lineage was free to fold, and the first run of
this rule drew the dominant lineages of the year as "others". Every live family
therefore keeps its top lineage on the chart, even if that leaves more than
ten.

Voc.pinned holds lineages that neither the floor's last two tests nor the cost
tier may remove -- the first two floor tests still apply, since an unusable fit
is unusable. Pins come from LEnum lists whose start date falls inside the
emitted window; both current lists start well before it, so the set is empty
today. The mechanism is there for the next time a list is added for a wave in
progress. Lapis's own MIN_SEQUENCES fold runs before the pins are taken and
ignores them; see docs/active/findings/ for the open entry on this.


WHERE A FIT STARTS
------------------
Every fit is a regression of log(sewage * prevalence) against day, and the
question is only where to start it. The seed is lastInflection: the latest peak
or valley of the sewage series this Voc is paired with, from
sewage/Abstract.java getLastInflection, clamped to leave at least MIN_FIT_DAYS
(28) of data. Each variant then tries every earlier start back to the first day
and keeps the one giving the lowest slope; it does not stop at the first rise.
A lineage with under two points from the seed on keeps the seed and fails the
floor for having no finite slope. Once merging is done, the Fit start marker on
the all-lineage charts and the collective fit's first day are the latest of
the variants' starts, not the seed; a per-lineage chart marks its own.

This used to be a hand-edited date, and by the time anyone looked it was
2-25-2024 -- earlier than the start of the emitted window, so every fit spanned
the entire year and every slope came out negative. Anchoring it to an
inflection the data itself produces is the fix, and is why the sewage subsystem
computes inflections for series nobody charts.


STRAINS
-------
variants/Strain.java buckets lineages into named strains by ancestry, for the
per-strain charts. Its buckets are hand-written and stop at BA.2.86; every
2026-era lineage falls through to OTHERS. See docs/active/findings/ for the
open entry on this.
